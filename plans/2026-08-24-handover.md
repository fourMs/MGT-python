### Two release-path faults, both now written down

**The publish workflow refused, correctly, and `RELEASING.md` did not say it would.** The workflow
opens with a job called *Refuse to publish from a commit CI has not passed*, and `in_progress` counts
as not passed. Pushing the commit and creating the Release in the same breath therefore fails: the
release exists, the run goes red at its first job, nothing is built and nothing reaches PyPI. That
happened to 1.14.0. The order is push, **wait for CI**, then `gh release create`; the repair is
`gh run rerun <id>`. `RELEASING.md` carries this now.

**The release guard had a bug in itself.** `test_every_changelog_version_has_a_tag` looked only for
`v`-prefixed tags. This project stopped using that prefix on 2026-08-16, so the check passed for
exactly as long as the newest release was the one it skips: 1.13.0 was tagged bare, and the test went
red the moment 1.14.0 sat above it, blaming a correctly tagged release. It accepts both conventions
now, verified by reverting to the v-only rule and watching it fail.

**And the changelog's compare links were dead for two releases.** `[Unreleased]` still compared from
`v1.12.1`, and 1.13.0 and 1.14.0 had no link reference at all. That test checks *tags*, not *links*,
so it could not have caught this.

## The fault that mattered: motion extraction was quadratic

`mg_motion` grew every accumulator --- `time`, `qom`, `com`, `aom` and both motiongrams ---
with `np.append` inside the frame loop. Each append copies the whole array, so cost is
O(n^2) in frames, and the motiongrams grow to `height x frames x 3` so it is quadratic in
bytes as well. **The numbers were right the whole time, which is why nothing caught it.**

Measured on 1080p: 69 s, 148 s and 366 s for 30 s, 60 s and 120 s, an exponent rising from
1.10 to 1.31. Extrapolated to this session: **about 215 hours**. After the fix: 64 s, 123 s,
242 s --- linear, about **5.3 h**, and **1.4 h** with `motion_analysis='qom',
save_motiongrams=False`. Output byte-identical, verified at all three lengths.

*The first regression test I wrote for it could not fail.* It timed a short clip against a
four-times-longer one; at a small frame size the quadratic term is still noise, so it passed
against the unfixed code. `tests/test_motion_scaling.py` counts `np.append` calls instead
and asserts the count does not grow with frames --- it fails on the original and passes on
the fix.

## Six ways of making it faster, five of them measured losses

ARJ asked whether ffmpeg could do more of the work. It cannot, and the table is the answer:

| approach | 120 s clip | agreement with QoM |
|---|---|---|
| **decode + numpy reduction** | **62 s** | exact |
| ffmpeg emits `gray` | slower pipe (15.7 vs 10.4 s) | exact |
| `signalstats` on MGT's chain | fast | r = 0.90 |
| `scale=1:H:flags=area` in ffmpeg | 106 s | exact |
| packet size from the bitstream | 0.1 s | **r = 0.33** |
| `gray,tblend,signalstats` | 118 s | r = 0.94 |

**ffmpeg wins at decoding and filtering and loses at reductions.** It stays where ARJ put
it --- it is also what gives MGT AVI, MOV, HEVC, GoPro and 360 for free --- and the
reduction stays in numpy. The compressed-domain proxy is free and useless here: the encoder
spends bits on visual complexity, not on movement. **Codec motion vectors
(`-flags2 +export_mvs`) are the one genuinely different approach not yet probed.**

## `_tracks.py` is on a branch and is NOT trusted

`tracks-wip`. A lean one-pass extractor: one greyscale conversion feeding qom and both
videogram columns, preallocated memmaps, a pyramid of extremes so the session zooms like an
audio waveform, and parallel chunks. 120 s of video in 60 s serially, 27 s over eight
processes.

**Its qom differs from `mg_motion`'s and differs between identical runs of itself.**
Non-determinism means frames are being misaligned in how it drives `ffmpeg_cmd`. Two real
bugs were found and fixed and neither was the cause: a `-t` binding to a `color=` input
instead of the output, and pipe reads not looped to a whole frame. The same commands
through plain `subprocess.Popen` produce identical frames, so the fault is on this side.

It also carries one mypy error, so it cannot merge until that is fixed too.

*Next step:* a test that fails on the current code --- extract two overlapping ranges and
assert the shared frames are equal. The non-determinism breaks that immediately, which is
the right place to start rather than from the idea.

## And a second fault of the same shape: long videos could not be OPENED

Found because the overnight run died instantly. `get_framecount` gave ffprobe a flat
**10-second timeout** and, on timeout, escalated to `-count_frames` --- which fully decodes
and is far slower --- so that timed out too and raised. A ceiling on video length with
nothing to do with any analysis.

Counting packets on the 2 h 38 min recording takes **22 s**, so `MgVideo()` raised
*"Could not count frames. (Is this a video file?)"* on an ordinary MP4. Anything past
roughly twenty minutes was unopenable. It opens in 21 s now and reports 475,680 frames.

The allowance scales with the file (`framecount_timeout()`, separate so a test can shorten
it and exercise the real path against a real ffprobe), and a timeout falls back to the
container's `nb_frames` with a `RuntimeWarning` instead of escalating. Four tests, which
error on the old code.

**These two bugs share a shape and that is the lesson.** Both were invisible on a clip and
fatal on a session, and neither changed a number. MGT's suite is entirely short-clip, so
nothing in it could have found either. The property tests added here --- count the
allocations, shorten the timeout --- are the cheap way to cover that class without a
two-hour fixture in CI.

## `_tracks.py`: the non-determinism is solved

`ffmpeg_cmd(pipe="read")` appends its OWN output arguments, and the module had appended its
own. ffmpeg was given two outputs and wrote both into one stdout, interleaved --- wrong
values, and different between runs because interleaving follows buffering. Serial extraction
is now byte-for-byte equal to `mg_motion` over 6,005 frames of 1080p.

**The parallel path is still not exact**: one repeated frame at the last chunk seam, because
`-ss` before `-i` lands on a keyframe and the frames decoded before the target are not always
the single one the worker drops. Seven interior seams are exact. Do not use it until that is.
